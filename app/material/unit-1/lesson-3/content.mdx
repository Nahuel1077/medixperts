  ### **Let’s explore the four main roles of a healthcare interpreter.**
<Paragraph
  h1="Read each flashcard carefully. Then, click on it to flip it over and reveal a practical example of how the interpreter applies that role during a medical encounter."

<Flashcard 
  title="The Conduit (The Default Role)"
  front="This is the role interpreters use most of the time.
In this role, your job is to interpret the message accurately and completely without adding, changing, or omitting information.
The interpreter serves as a communication bridge between the patient and the healthcare provider."
  back="🩺  Doctor 
 Good morning. Are you currently taking any medication?

🎧  Interpreter 
 Buenos días. ¿Está tomando algún medicamento actualmente?

👤  Patient
 Sí, tomo medicamento para la presión alta.

🎧  Interpreter 
 Yes, I take medication for high blood pressure."
 />

<Flashcard 
  title="The Clarifier"
  front="Sometimes, a word, phrase, or medical term may be confusing for the patient or difficult to interpret clearly.
In these situations, the interpreter may briefly ask for clarification to ensure the message is understood accurately.
The goal is not to explain the information personally, but to help avoid misunderstandings during communication."
  back="🩺  Doctor
 You must remain NPO after midnight.

🎧  Interpreter
 The interpreter requests clarification for the acronym NPO.

🩺  Doctor:
 It means you cannot eat or drink anything after midnight.

🎧  Interpreter
 No puede comer ni beber nada después de la medianoche."
 />

<Flashcard 
  title="The Cultural Broker"
  front="Sometimes, cultural beliefs or expressions may be unfamiliar to the healthcare provider and create confusion during communication.
In these situations, the interpreter may briefly provide neutral cultural context to help both parties understand each other more clearly.
Although this role may seem subtly similar to the Clarifier role, the Cultural Broker role focuses specifically on culturally related beliefs, expressions, or concepts that may not be immediately understood during the interaction.
This role should be used carefully and only when cultural differences directly affect communication."
  back="🩺  Doctor: Have you taken any medication for your stomach pain?

🎧  Interpreter: ¿Ha tomado algún medicamento para el dolor de estómago?

👤  Patient: No, creo que tengo empacho.

🎧  Interpreter: The patient mentioned “empacho,” a cultural term commonly used to describe stomach discomfort or digestive issues.

🩺  Doctor: I see. Thank you.

🎧  Interpreter: Entiendo. Gracias."
 />

<Flashcard 
  title="The Advocate (The Exception)"
  front="This is the most active and least common role of the healthcare interpreter.
In rare situations involving immediate patient safety, serious ethical concerns, or clear communication breakdowns, the interpreter may need to speak up to help prevent harm.
Because this role goes beyond the interpreter’s usual neutral position, it should only be used as a last resort in extreme situations where no other reasonable option is available and according to company policies or professional protocols."
  back="🩺  Doctor: You can take penicillin twice a day for the next 7 days.
🎧  Interpreter: Puede tomar penicilina dos veces al día durante los próximos 7 días.
👤  Patient: Pero soy alérgico a la penicilina. La última vez me salió una erupción muy fuerte.
🎧  Interpreter: But I’m allergic to penicillin. Last time I developed a severe rash.
🩺  Doctor: Alright, I’ll send the prescription now.
🎧  Interpreter: The interpreter would like to point out that the patient mentioned being allergic to penicillin.
🩺  Doctor: Oh, thank you for catching that. Let’s change the medication then.
🎧  Interpreter: Gracias por señalarlo. Vamos a cambiar el medicamento entonces."
 />
