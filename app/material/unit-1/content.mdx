<Presentation

list={["Lesson 1: What Medical Interpreting Is (and Is NOT):"]}

Delivered in video format, this lesson defines medical interpreting as an accurate, neutral, and real-time linguistic bridge that guarantees patient communicative autonomy and healthcare equity. It debunks the myth of 'bilingualism equals professional skill' by detailing what interpreting is not—namely, casual conversation, clinical advice, or message filtering.

<Presentation
list={["Lesson 2: Difference Between Translation and Interpretation:"]}
/>
A video-based lesson featuring interactive scenario practices that distinguishes written translation (text-to-text, allowing research and revision) from oral interpretation (spoken/signed, immediate, real-time, and live).

<Presentation
list={["Lesson 3: The Interpreter’s Role in Healthcare Settings:"]}
/>
A text and flashcard-based guide introducing the four distinct roles of an interpreter, ordered from least to most intrusive: Conduit (the default, exact rendering), Clarifier (addressing confusion and acronyms), Cultural Broker (mediating cultural beliefs), and Advocate (an exceptional intervention used strictly to protect immediate patient safety).

<Presentation
list={["Lesson 4: Modes of Interpreting:"]} 
/>
This video lesson explores the four primary interpreting modalities in clinical settings: Consecutive (the standard turn-taking mode), Simultaneous (real-time delivery used in emergencies), Sight Translation (oral rendering of written documents like consent forms), and Summarization (strictly a last resort).
