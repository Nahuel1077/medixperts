<Title
title="2. Code of Ethics vs. Standards of Practice"
p="These terms are often used together. However, they are not exactly the same."
dictionary={[
    {
    element:"● Code of Ethics (The 'What')",
    explanation:"The Code of Ethics establishes the fundamental principles that guide every professional interpreter's decisions. These principles define what is considered ethical and appropriate during a medical encounter. Think of it as the set of principles that helps you determine the most appropriate course of action whenever you're faced with an ethical decision during an interpreting encounter.",
    },
    {
    element:"● Standards of Practice (The 'How')",
    explanation:"The Standards of Practice explain how interpreters apply those ethical principles during real medical encounters. They describe the professional behaviors, protocols, and decision-making processes that help interpreters put ethics into practice, such as requesting clarification, intervening appropriately, managing communication challenges, or recognizing when an intervention is necessary.",
    },
    ]}
/>
<Paragraph
h1="In other words, the Code of Ethics explains what professional interpreters are expected to do, while the Standards of Practice explain how those principles are applied during real medical encounters. Throughout this unit, our primary focus will be on some of the most important ethical principles that guide every professional medical interpreter."

<Title
title="3. Core Ethical Principles"
p="Although the terminology may vary slightly from one organization to another, professional medical interpreting is generally built upon very similar ethical principles.
Some of the most important principles are:"
dictionary={[
    {element:"🎯 Accuracy", explanation:"Faithfully communicate the speaker's intended meaning without adding, omitting, summarizing, or changing the message."},
    {element:"🔒 Confidentiality", explanation:"Protect all patient information and respect the privacy of everyone involved in the medical encounter."},
    {element:"🚧 Role Boundaries", explanation:"Understand the limits of your professional role. Interpreters facilitate communication—they do not provide medical advice, make decisions for patients, or assume responsibilities that belong to healthcare professionals."},
    {element:"🌎 Cultural Awareness", explanation:"Recognize that patients may communicate differently because of their language, culture, education, personal experiences, or social background. Cultural awareness helps interpreters understand these differences while remaining respectful and professional."},
    {element:"⚖️ Impartiality", explanation:"Remain neutral, objective, and unbiased throughout every interpreting encounter, regardless of your personal beliefs, opinions, or emotions."},
    {element:"🛡️ Advocacy", explanation:"Advocacy should only be considered in exceptional situations where patient safety or well-being is at immediate risk and no other communication strategy is sufficient."},
]}
