<Paragraph

<Paragraph

<Paragraph

/>

<Paragraph
title="Stage 3: The Introduction to the LEP"
p="After identifying yourself as the interpreter for the requested non-English language, you are typically given the opportunity to introduce yourself directly to the LEP as well."
/>
Standard Script:
<Paragraph
highlight="'Saludos. Mi nombre es [Your Name], y seré su intérprete hoy. Todo lo que se hable en este encuentro se mantendrá de manera confidencial y será interpretado en primera persona.'"
/>

<Paragraph
cursive="NOTE: Just like in stage two, the introduction directed to the LEP may also vary depending on the company you work for. Certain pieces of information may be modified, added, or omitted according to specific client preferences, company policies, or operational requirements."
/>

<Paragraph
title="Stage 4: The Interpreting Encounter"
p="This is the core of the call, where consecutive interpreting takes place. You guide the flow of communication, may briefly take on supportive roles such as clarifying when needed, and use temporary notes to help accurately retain and render medical information."
/>

<Paragraph
title="Stage 5: The Closing & Outbound Greeting"
p="Once the provider indicates that the session is ending, you are generally expected to confirm whether any additional assistance is needed before disconnecting. If the provider confirms that no further assistance is required, you then proceed with the standard closing script and end the call."
/>
Standard Script:
<Paragraph
highlight="'Is there anything else I can assist you with today? ... Thank you for calling [Company Name]. Interpreter 12345 will disconnect from the call. Have a wonderful day. El intérprete se desconectará de la llamada. Adiós.'"
/>
<Paragraph
cursive="NOTE: Just like the introductions given to both parties, the closing of a call may vary depending on client requirements and the information the interpreter is expected to provide. In some companies, closing scripts may even differ between OPI and VRI encounters."
